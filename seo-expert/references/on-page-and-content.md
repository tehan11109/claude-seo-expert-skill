# On-Page SEO & Content (sources: Backlinko, Surfer, Yoast, Google)

## On-page checklist
- **Title tag:** unique, accurate, roughly 50–60 characters (Google truncates by pixel width), with the main keyword near the start and the brand at the end if there's room. Make it compelling, not clickbait.
- **Meta description:** unique summary of about 150–160 characters, including the topic and a reason to click. Google may rewrite it, which is fine.
- **H1:** one clear H1 that matches the intent, usually close to the title.
- **Headings:** logical H2/H3 hierarchy. Descriptive H2s that each answer a sub-question help both skimmers and AI passage retrieval.
- **Keyword use:** the primary term early in the content (first ~100 words), in the H1, in at least one H2, and in the slug. Use variants and related terms naturally. No stuffing.
- **URL slug:** short, readable, keyword-descriptive, with no dates for evergreen content.
- **Intro:** answer or promise the answer immediately. Put the headline and intro above the fold, without a giant hero image pushing content down.
- **Formatting:** short paragraphs, lists, tables, comparison blocks and visuals.
- **Images:** descriptive file names and alt text, compressed (WebP/AVIF), with width and height set, lazy-loaded below the fold.
- **Internal links:** from strong, relevant pages to the page you want to rank, using descriptive anchors. Add links from new posts to money pages.
- **External links:** cite authoritative sources where they help the reader.
- **Structured data:** Article, Product, FAQ (rich results limited), LocalBusiness, BreadcrumbList, Organization, Review, Video. It must match the visible content.
- **Freshness:** update when facts change. Don't fake dates.
- **CTR:** monitor in Search Console. Low CTR at a good position means you should rewrite the title and description.

## Content quality: beyond the checklist
- Content should show **experience**: real usage, photos, results, mistakes, local specifics. This is the hardest thing for competitors (and AI) to copy.
- **Original value:** data, case studies, expert quotes, tools, templates, clearer explanations.
- **Authorship:** a real byline linked to an author page with credentials. An About page and contact details build trust.
- Accuracy matters most on YMYL topics. Cite sources.

## Semantic SEO & entities (Surfer)
- Google understands **entities** (people, places, brands, concepts) and the relationships between them, not just strings of text.
- Cover the topic completely. Include the subtopics, questions and related entities that the top pages consistently address. A content gap or NLP term tool can help, but treat its "score" as a guide, not a target.
- Use precise, unambiguous wording. Name the entities explicitly ("Colombo", "Ceylon tea", "Shopify Payments") instead of vague pronouns.
- **Topical authority:** a site that covers a subject deeply and coherently, with many interlinked pages, tends to rank better within that subject than one-off posts. Build clusters, not random articles.

## Topic clusters & site structure (Yoast)
- **Pillar / cornerstone page:** your best, most complete page on a core topic, with the most internal links pointing to it.
- **Cluster pages:** each targets one subtopic and links up to the pillar and sideways to related clusters.
- **Flat structure:** important pages should be reachable within about 3 clicks of the homepage. Use categories and tags sparingly and meaningfully.
- **Breadcrumbs:** for usability and to give Google structural context.
- **Orphan pages** (no internal links) rarely perform well. Find and link them, or remove them.

## Content maintenance
- **Content pruning:** for pages with no traffic, no links and no purpose, improve them, merge them into a stronger page with a 301, or remove them (410/404) if they're truly worthless.
- **Content refresh:** pages that are declining in Search Console are prime candidates. Update the facts, add missing subtopics and fix the intent match.
- Avoid near-duplicate pages (e.g. city pages with only the name swapped). These are doorway risks.
