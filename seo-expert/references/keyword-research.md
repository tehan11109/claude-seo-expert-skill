# Keyword Research & Search Intent (sources: Ahrefs, Moz, LearningSEO)

## Process
1. **Seed keywords.** Brainstorm what customers would type: products, problems and jargon. Ask the user how their customers describe things, in their own language. Sri Lankan users, for example, often search in English, Sinhala, Tamil or "Singlish" transliteration.
2. **Mine what already works.** Search Console → Performance → Queries. Look for queries with high impressions and low CTR, and queries ranking at positions 5–20. These are the fastest wins.
3. **Competitor keywords.** Find who ranks for the seeds, then look at their top pages and do a content gap analysis (keywords they rank for and you don't).
4. **Listen to real conversations.** Reddit, Facebook groups, forums, Quora, YouTube comments, "People also ask", autocomplete and "related searches" all show the real wording people use.
5. **Expand** with tools: Google Keyword Planner (free), Search Console, Ahrefs/Semrush/Moz, AnswerThePublic, Google Trends (for seasonality and regional comparison).

## Metrics: how to read them honestly
- **Search volume** counts searches, not people, and is a rounded estimate. Even the #1 result gets only part of it.
- **Traffic potential** is better. It estimates the traffic the top-ranking page gets across *all* the keywords it ranks for.
- **Keyword difficulty** is a tool's guess (often based on referring domains to the top 10). Check manually: who ranks, how strong they are, and how good their content is. A high KD can still be worth it if the topic attracts links.
- **Business potential** (0–3):
  - 3 = your product is essential to solving the query
  - 2 = it helps
  - 1 = it can be mentioned
  - 0 = no fit
  - Prioritize 2s and 3s.
- All numbers are country-specific. Low-volume markets like Sri Lanka often show "0–10" for keywords that still convert. Don't dismiss them.

## Search intent
Buckets: **informational** (learn), **commercial** (compare or evaluate), **transactional** (buy or act), **navigational** (find a specific site), plus **local** (near me / city). Many queries are mixed.

**The live SERP is the truth.** Google shows what it thinks the intent is. Analyze the top 10 with the **3 Cs**:
- **Content type**: blog post, product page, category page, landing page, video, tool
- **Content format**: how-to, listicle, guide, comparison, review, template
- **Content angle**: the shared hook: "for beginners", "2026", "cheap", "in Sri Lanka", "without X"

Match the type and format, and pick a compatible angle. Then add something unique. If rankings for the query are volatile, the intent is unclear or shifting.

## Grouping & mapping
- **Parent topic / clustering:** keywords whose top results overlap heavily belong on *one* page. Don't create separate pages for close variants.
- **Keyword mapping:** assign one primary cluster per URL, recorded in a sheet (Keyword | Intent | Cluster | URL | Status).
- **Cannibalization:** two of your own URLs competing for the same query, which often shows as their rankings flip-flopping in Search Console. Fix it by merging and 301-redirecting, differentiating the intent, or canonicalizing.
- **Topic clusters:** a pillar page covers the broad topic, and supporting pages cover subtopics. Link them all both ways (see `on-page-and-content.md`).

## Prioritization
Balance quick wins (low difficulty, existing pages at positions 5–20) with medium-term clusters and long-term competitive head terms. If the user needs leads soon, start with high-business-potential, lower-competition commercial and local keywords.
