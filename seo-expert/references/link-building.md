# Link Building & Authority (sources: Ahrefs, Moz, Backlinko, Google spam policies)

## What makes a link valuable
- **Relevance:** a site or page about a related topic.
- **Authority:** a trusted, well-linked site. DR (Ahrefs), DA (Moz) and AS (Semrush) are *third-party estimates*, not Google metrics, so use them only to compare.
- **Placement:** inside the main content, editorially given, and ideally high on the page. Footer, sidebar and sitewide links are worth little.
- **Anchor text:** natural and varied (brand, URL, descriptive phrases). Heavily keyword-matched anchor profiles look manipulative.
- **Real traffic:** a link people actually click is a strong sign it's genuine.
- **Follow vs. nofollow/sponsored/ugc:** paid or sponsored links *must* be marked `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`.

## Ethical tactics that work
- **Linkable assets:** original research, surveys, local data (e.g. "Sri Lanka e-commerce statistics"), free tools and calculators, templates, definitive guides. Promote them, because people can't link to what they haven't seen.
- **Digital PR:** pitch newsworthy data or stories to journalists. Respond to source requests (Qwoted, Featured, Help a B2B Writer, and local journalists).
- **Guest posting:** write for genuinely relevant publications for their audience, not just for links. Pitch topics they lack.
- **Broken link building:** find dead resources that still have backlinks, create a better replacement and tell the linking sites.
- **Skyscraper / content upgrade:** improve on a popular but outdated resource, then contact the sites that link to it.
- **Unlinked brand mentions:** ask authors who already mention you to add a link.
- **Resource pages:** request inclusion where you're a genuine fit.
- **Image link reclamation:** ask sites that use your images without credit to add one.
- **Competitor backlink gap:** find sites linking to several competitors but not you.
- **Local & business citations:** chambers of commerce, industry associations, local directories, suppliers and partners, sponsorships, university and community events.
- **Relationships first:** engage with people in your niche before you ask. Contributors quoted in your content often share it.

## Outreach basics
Personalize, keep it short, offer clear value to *their* readers, follow up once, and track everything in a sheet. Expect low reply rates, so make each email relevant rather than sending more of them.

## Internal link equity
Money pages (products, services) rarely earn links themselves. Earn links to your assets, then link internally from those assets to your money pages.

## Don'ts (link spam = spam policy violation)
Buying or selling followed links, PBNs, link exchanges at scale, automated or link-farm links, keyword-stuffed widget or footer links, and spammy forum or comment links.

## Disavow
Use it only for a manual action for unnatural links, or a clear large-scale history of paid or spammy links you created. Google ignores most random spam links anyway, and disavowing good links by mistake hurts. Audit before using it.

## Measuring
Track referring domains over time, links to key pages, and branded search growth. Brand demand is a strong signal of real authority.
